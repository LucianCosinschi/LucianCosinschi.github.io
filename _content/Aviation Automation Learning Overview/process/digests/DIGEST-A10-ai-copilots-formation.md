## The core argument this stream supports

Assisted performance and unassisted capability **dissociate**, practitioners **cannot detect the dissociation in themselves**, and the destination fields are **re-deriving automation complacency from scratch without citing aviation**. That last point is the report's justification: Dell'Acqua reaches for self-driving cars, the BCG paper reaches for Lebovitz et al. (2022) — neither cites Bainbridge, Wiener, Billings or Sarter & Woods.

## Strongest citations, with design and N

**1. Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö. & Mariman, R. (2024/25). "Generative AI Can Harm Learning." SSRN WP 4895486.** *Cluster-RCT (classroom-randomised), pre-registered (AsPredicted 4DL_Q3J), N = 839 students, ~50 classes, Turkish high school, 4×90-min maths sessions.* **Working paper — not peer reviewed.**
Practice phase: GPT Base +0.137 (SE .031) = **+48%**; GPT Tutor +0.361 (SE .032) = **+127%**. Unassisted exam: GPT Base **−0.054 (SE .022), p<.05 = −17%**; GPT Tutor **−0.004 (n.s.)**.
**Report both arms.** The Tutor result is a **null, not a win** — safeguards prevented damage, they did not produce learning. Mechanism: GPT Base wrong 49% of the time; 31% of first messages were "what is the answer?"; logical errors did *not* transfer to exam problems (non-learning shown via a null). **Metacognitive result:** harmed students did not perceive reduced learning. Published critique exists: Tan & Rajaratnam, SSRN 4898213.

**2. Liu, G., Christian, B., Dumbalska, T., Bakker, M. & Dubey, R. (2026). "AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance." arXiv:2604.04721v2.** *3 RCTs, Prolific, combined N = 1,222 (307/585/168).* **Preprint.**
Withdrawal-phase solve rates, d = **−0.42 / −0.19 / −0.42**. Novel contribution is **persistence**: skip rate d = 0.25 (Exp 1) and 0.42 (Exp 3). Costs concentrated in the direct-solutions subgroup (cross-sectional, authors say not necessarily causal). Exposure was **10–15 minutes** — the "AI damages your brain in 10 minutes" framing is a distortion.

**3. Dell'Acqua, F., McFowland III, E., Mollick, E. et al. (2026). "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier." Organization Science 37(2), DOI 10.1287/orsc.2025.21838.** *Pre-registered field experiment, N = 758 BCG consultants.* **Now peer reviewed** — most commentary still cites the working paper.
Inside frontier: +12.2% tasks, 25.1% faster, >40% quality. **Outside frontier: control correct 84.5%, AI conditions 60% and 70% — 19pp decrease.** Skill compression: bottom-half +43%, top-half +17%.
**Correction for the brief:** "falling asleep at the wheel" is **not** an argument in this paper — it appears only as a reference to Dell'Acqua's own separate study.

**4. Dell'Acqua, F. (2022). "Falling Asleep at the Wheel: Human/AI Collaboration in a Field Experiment on HR Recruiters." HBS/LISH, pre-registered (OSF qp8et).** *RCT, N = 181 recruiters, 5,184 analysed evaluations, 4 arms (99%/85%/75%/no AI), PIAAC ground truth.* **Working paper.**
**Recruiters with the *worse* (75%) AI outperformed those with the better (85%) AI** (p = .05 on the 1–10 scale). Effort mechanism measured: Bad AI **+8.77 sec** per evaluation (p<.01); Good AI −1.53 (n.s.). **Theorem 1: combined human–AI accuracy is U-shaped in algorithmic precision.** Experienced recruiters gained from bad AI and were *harmed* by good AI. This is the formal statement of what aviation learned empirically — **pair it with Parasuraman & Riley.**

**5. Shen, J. H. & Tamkin, A. (2026). "How AI Impacts Skill Formation." arXiv:2601.20245v1.** *Pre-registered RCT (OSF pk6a5), N = 52 professional programmers.* **Preprint; both authors at Anthropic — discloses against interest.**
Withdrawal design: AI arm **17% lower** on unassisted quiz, **d = 0.738, p = .010**. **No significant speed gain** — so not even a speed/learning trade-off. **Mechanism: controls hit 3× more errors (median 3 vs 1), correlated with score.** Six interaction patterns; *AI Delegation* = fastest completion, worst learning.

**6. Scarfe, P., Watcham, K., Clarke, A. & Roesch, E. (2024). PLOS ONE 19(6):e0305354.** *Covert real-world field study, peer reviewed. 33 AI accounts, 5 modules, ~5% of submissions.*
**94% undetected** (97% on stricter criterion). AI scored **~5pp above real-student medians**; **83.4%** probability of outscoring a random real submission. Authors note they submitted **unmodified** output — "the most detectable way possible." Converts a contested claim into a measured fact.

**7. Koedinger, K. R. & Aleven, V. (2007). "Exploring the Assistance Dilemma in Experiments with Cognitive Tutors." Educational Psychology Review 19(3), 239–264.** *Peer-reviewed review/synthesis — by far the most established scholarship in this stream.*
"How should learning environments balance information or assistance **giving** and **withholding**?" The answer is conditional on prior knowledge, task complexity, and **strategic fading of scaffolding over time**. **Fading = currency.** Same construct, two fields, zero cross-citation.

**8. Boyd, V. A., Whitehead, C. R., Thille, P., Ginsburg, S., Brydges, R. & Kuper, A. (2018). "CBME: the discourse of infallibility." Medical Education 52(1), 45–57.** *Foucauldian critical discourse analysis — non-empirical.*
CBME proponents deflect conceptual criticism as implementation failure, insulating the framework. Reductionism; behaviourist foundations limiting "reflection, intuition, experience"; **no method exists to assess the six ACGME competencies as independent constructs**; "paucity of empirical evidence." Corroborated by Alharbi (2024), *BMC Med Educ* 24:612 — systematized review, 38 articles: **84% didn't report evaluation approach, 63% omitted standards.**

**Also load-bearing:** ten Cate (2005), *Med Educ* 39(12):1176–7 — note it is a **two-page commentary**. Five-level entrustment–supervision scale (ten Cate et al. 2020, NCBI NBK619392) — the key move is **prospective/decisional** ("ready for more autonomy?") vs **retrospective/evaluative** ("how good were they?"). TEQSA (Lodge, Howard, Bearman, Dawson, 2023): **Proposition 5 — "security at meaningful points across a program"** is the higher-ed restatement of the checkride. Kizilcec, Chirikov & Smirnov (2026), *Science* 392:818–820 — survey, **N > 95,000** undergraduates, 20 universities: 37% monthly use, 9% admitted cheating, **26% of daily users vs 7% of monthly users** (self-report; 9% is a floor).

## Two things aviation has that nothing else does

**Recurrent proficiency checking.** CBME builds competence and has essentially **no machinery for detecting its decay**. A pilot re-demonstrates competence every six months for forty years; a doctor does it once. If AI erosion is gradual, initial certification is structurally incapable of catching it.

**The simulator.** The withdrawal condition — the highest-value evidence design — is only routinely available where safe simulation exists. **If you cannot construct a setting in which the assistant is absent, you cannot assess or maintain unassisted capability.** That is a product requirement, not a nicety.

**Convergence worth using:** aviation ("secure the checkride, free the practice"), TEQSA Proposition 5, and medicine's programmatic assessment independently reached the same architecture — formative unsecured practice punctuated by few, secure, consequential gates. Three fields, no cross-citation, same answer.

## Findings I downgraded

- **"Your Brain on ChatGPT" (Kosmyna et al., arXiv 2506.08872).** N = 54 (18/group; **only 18 completed the crossover**). Published commentary (Stanković et al., arXiv:2601.00856): needs ~**N=159** for power; FDR level never stated; 1,000 per-electrode ANOVAs with unstated selection criteria; 2–4 essays per group in some analyses; the **Search Engine group showed no impairment** and this was underplayed. Contributes nothing the better studies don't.
- **METR 19% slowdown** (Becker et al., arXiv:2507.09089; N = **16 developers**, 246 issues). CI **+2% to +39%**. METR's **Feb 2026 post** reports the follow-up found the *opposite sign* (−18%/−4%) and disowns both datasets for selection — 30–50% of developers withheld tasks they expected AI to help with. **Cite only the perception gap:** expected +24%, measured −19%, still believed +20% afterwards. That ~39-point gap survives the critique.
- **Peng et al. (2023) 55.8%.** 95% CI **[21%, 89%]**; 95 randomised but **only 70 analysed**; authors at **GitHub Inc. and Microsoft Research**; single greenfield HTTP-server task; authors state it "does not examine code quality."
- **"Fast and Forgettable" (Gardella et al., arXiv:2604.18538).** N = **22**, convenience sample. Key retention finding **fails multiple-comparison correction (adj. p = .054)**; absolute retest difference **null (p = .397)**. The robust part is the **workload** result (large drops in mental demand and effort).
- **Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen "Canaries."** ADP payroll, observational. 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed roles **19% below** trend, operating **through reduced hiring not separations**. Authors call these "**descriptive indicators… rather than causal estimates**," note attenuation when controlling for education, and in Feb 2026 **revised the timing** — significant only **after 2024** under firm–time fixed effects.
- **GitClear code-quality reports.** **Vendor, observational, non-peer-reviewed.** Cite as an industry signal only. That the best quality evidence is vendor-produced is itself a finding.
- **Attribution corrections:** Burning Glass *The Expertise Upheaval* is by **Melissa DiMarzio**, not Sigelman (Sigelman owns the "start careers in the middle" framing). The SSIR entry-level piece is **Bruno V. Manno**, "A New AI Career Ladder," 30 Oct 2025.

## Gaps — put these in the report

1. **No long-run withdrawal study exists.** Max exposure anywhere: four sessions. Max retention interval: one week, N = 22, underpowered. Career-scale degradation is an **extrapolation**.
2. **Nobody has demonstrated the pipeline consequence** — that juniors deprived of junior work fail to become seniors. Supported by mechanism and analogy, not outcome data. That test takes a decade.
3. **No study shows an AI assistant producing *better* durable capability than unassisted practice.** The best available result is a null (Bastani's Tutor arm). Any "AI tutors work if designed right" claim over-reads the evidence.
4. **Almost nothing is replicated**, and two of the most-cited results were **qualified by their own authors** within a year.
5. **The market runs on engagement, not learning.** Kucirkova et al. (2025), *Hum Soc Sci Commun* 12:947 (peer-reviewed Delphi): educational impact "has not traditionally been a primary consideration in… procurement"; investors "prioritised scaling and user acquisition"; **75 competing evaluation frameworks**. Combined with Bastani's metacognitive finding — **learners rate the harmful condition favourably** — a satisfaction-optimising product will optimise toward the GPT Base arm. This is Bainbridge's irony restated commercially.
6. **Telemetry has no unassisted condition.** It structurally cannot measure the thing that matters, so a product can look excellent on every metric while producing the harmful outcome.
