# Digest — A5: The regulatory and training response

Companion to `A5-regulatory-training-response.md`.

---

## Findings

**The core move.** In 1990 the FAA did something structurally novel: it offered operators freedom from prescribed training content in exchange for proof. **SFAR 58** (55 FR 40262, 2 Oct 1990, Docket 25-04) required only that an AQP provide "at least an equivalent means of compliance with current regulations." Operators gained proficiency-based rather than hours-based qualification and wrote their own Terminal Proficiency Objectives via Job Task Analysis; in return they accepted permanent data-surrender obligations. SFAR 58 migrated into **14 CFR Part 121 Subpart Y** (§§121.901–121.925; source credit Docket FAA-2005-20750, 70 FR 54815, 16 Sept 2005), with **AC 120-54A** (23 June 2006, Chg 1 31 Jan 2017) supplying the five-phase design machinery. AQP's "First Look" — evaluating objectives *before* any briefing or practice — is the direct ancestor of the EBT evaluation phase. Notably, AQP's chief documented evidential yield was as an *input to EBT*, not as a self-evaluation: rigorous published outcome evaluations of AQP are thin.

**MPL** arrived via **ICAO Annex 1 Amendment 167**, adopted by Council 10 March 2006, applicable 23 November 2006 — the first ICAO licence CBTA-compliant from the ground up, with TEM embedded from day one and a 240-hour minimum. The evidence is asymmetric: advocates cite training-system data (586 trainees, ~100% pass rates, average 105 sectors to line release; IATA end-2013: 800 graduates, ~30 airlines), with no control group. Critics — ECA (19 Dec 2013) and IFALPA — object that "the competency-based training concept was misunderstood or misinterpreted," producing hours-reduction exercises wearing competency labels. The FAA does not issue MPLs.

**What "evidence" means in EBT.** The point the wider L&D world routinely inverts. **Evidence-based does not mean "there is evidence the training works." It means the curriculum content and its frequency were derived from operational data rather than tradition.** ICAO Doc 9995: "Training and assessment **based on operational data**…" IATA: "The training topics and their associated frequency were defined during the EBT design phase, through the analysis of both safety and training data." The evidence sits *upstream* of the curriculum, not downstream of the outcome.

**The evidence base itself.** The **IATA Data Report for Evidence-Based Training is 1st Edition, August 2014** (ISBN 978-92-9252-228-5) — **not 2013**, correcting a widespread error. Doc 9995 (2013) preceded full publication of its own underpinning data. Seven data streams, 18 sources: LOSA Archive **9,000+ observations across 45 airlines**; **3,045 accidents/incidents 1962–2010** (NTSB primary); **1.7m+ flights** of FDA for Gen 3/4; **966** pilot survey respondents via IFALPA; **167** pilots in the Training Criticality Survey (161 three-part questions, 40 threats/errors, 51 types); 300+ evidence statements. Six aircraft generations (four jet, two turboprop). Threats scored on likelihood × severity × training benefit, yielding priority A/B/C over a three-year rolling programme.

Headline findings: manual aircraft control a factor in **52% of fatal accidents** and **84% of events highly mitigable by training**; pilots detect only ~**40%** of handling errors (commanders detect 39% of their FO's, **9% of their own**); **97%** of unstable approaches end in landing, **90% uneventfully**, while go-arounds from unstable approaches produce **85% more** high-severity events; **28%** of LOSA flights carry an automation error; procedures deficiency a factor in **49%** of highly-trainable accidents. The cleanest causal argument in the corpus: flights rated outstanding for Captain Leadership and Communication average **2.3 errors** versus **7.0** for poorly rated flights — *at identical threat load* (4.9 vs 5.0).

**The ICAO pilot core competencies.** Original set (Doc 9995 / EBT Implementation Guide 2013) = **eight**: Application of Procedures; Communication; Aeroplane Flight Path Management, automation; Aeroplane Flight Path Management, manual control; Leadership and Teamwork; Problem Solving and Decision-Making; Situation Awareness; Workload Management.

Revised set (ICAO 2020, PANS-TRG Amendment 7) = **nine**. Three changes, and precision matters: **"Application of Knowledge" was added**; "Application of Procedures" **became** "Application of Procedures **and Compliance with Regulations**"; "Situation Awareness" **became** "Situation Awareness **and Management of Information**." An addition plus two renamings/expansions — **not a split**. Decisive internal evidence: the new competency's observable behaviours are numbered **OB 0.1–0.7**, prepended to the existing 1–8 series.

The nine, with EASA abbreviations:

- **Application of Knowledge (KNO)** — demonstrates knowledge and understanding of relevant information, operating instructions, aircraft systems and the operating environment (OB 0.1–0.7)
- **Application of Procedures and Compliance with Regulations (PRO)** — identifies and applies appropriate procedures per published operating instructions and applicable regulations (OB 1.1–1.7; incl. 1.3 "Follows SOPs unless a higher degree of safety dictates an appropriate deviation")
- **Communication (COM)** — communicates through appropriate means in normal and non-normal situations (OB 2.1–2.10; incl. 2.7 escalation to resolve deviations)
- **Aeroplane Flight Path Management, automation (FPA)** — controls the flight path through automation (OB 3.1–3.6)
- **Aeroplane Flight Path Management, manual control (FPM)** — controls the flight path through manual control (OB 4.1–4.7)
- **Leadership and Teamwork (LTW)** — influences others toward a shared purpose; collaborates to accomplish team goals (OB 5.1–5.11)
- **Problem Solving and Decision-Making (PSD)** — identifies precursors, mitigates problems, makes decisions (OB 6.1–6.9; incl. 6.8 "Adapts when faced with situations where no guidance or procedure exists," 6.9 "Demonstrates resilience when encountering an unexpected event")
- **Situation Awareness and Management of Information (SAW)** — perceives, comprehends and manages information and anticipates its effect (OB 7.1–7.7)
- **Workload Management (WLM)** — maintains available workload capacity by prioritizing and distributing tasks (OB 8.1–8.9)

**EASA's rules.** ED Decision 2015/027/R introduced *mixed* EBT. **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/2036 of 9 December 2020** (OJ L 416/24, 11.12.2020) inserted **ORO.FC.231** (six modules over three years; minimum two per type-rating validity, ≥3 months apart), **ORO.FC.146** (instructor standardisation), **ORO.FC.232**, and **ARO.OPS.226**. **ED Decision 2021/002/R** issued the AMC/GM under **RMT.0599**. *Baseline* EBT **replaces the LPC and OPC**; *mixed* EBT retains them as a safety net. Each module has three phases — **Evaluation** (diagnostic, uninterrupted, identifies individual training needs), **Manoeuvres Training**, **Scenario-Based Training** (largest). The evaluation phase is not a jeopardy check because jeopardy has been *relocated* to a programme-level competency judgement and *decoupled* from the diagnostic observation — that decoupling is the actual innovation. Grading is 1–5 across four metric levels (competent / competency / observable behaviour / TEM).

**The instructor problem.** Instructor becomes facilitator (IOB 3.9 "Encourages the trainee to self-assess"; 3.10 "Allows trainee to self-correct"). EASA mandates an **Instructor Concordance Assurance Programme**: standardisation material every ≤72 months, grading-data analysis every 12 months, Cohen's/Fleiss' kappa and ICC. It exists because the underlying problem is real and unsolved: Weber, Roth, Mavin & Dekker (2013) found one rater pair passing a captain whom two pairs failed, dimension scores differing by **2.5 points on a 5-point scale**, and only 15–33% overlap in assessment topics — with the warning that "high IRR-scores do not imply that assessors made the same observations." The one study reporting strong agreement (Sun et al. 2023, ρ = 0.947) achieved it by substituting a flight-data algorithm for the human rater.

**Does EBT work?** **No independent peer-reviewed outcome evaluation of EBT versus the OPC was located.** EASA's own RMT.0599 safety material presents no accident statistics, no safety-benefit estimate, no OPC/EBT comparison. IATA's 2024 white paper argues deductively ("should enhance," "should lead to"). The strongest quantitative claims come from a **vendor** review (Salient, EATS Nov 2025: 90 airlines, check failures 3.4%→0.4% at one carrier) — no control, no peer review. EBT is evidence-based in curriculum derivation and largely faith-based in outcome validation.

**UPRT.** ICAO **Doc 10011** (1st ed. 2014) defines upset as pitch >25° nose-up, >10° nose-down, bank >45°, or inappropriate airspeed within those bounds; it emerged from the RAeS **ICATEE** work after a June 2009 conference. US: **PL 111-216 §208** (post-Colgan 3407) → **78 FR 67800**, 12 Nov 2013, **Amdt 121-366**, **§121.423 Extended Envelope Training** (six tasks; the decisive shift from "approach to stall" to actual **stall recovery**) → **81 FR 18178**, 30 Mar 2016, **Amdt 60-4**, requiring stall modelling to **10° AOA beyond stall identification**. The FAA candidly concedes fidelity limits: type-specific validated behaviour beyond stall "may not be a reasonable goal," permitting "type representative" modelling with documented Statements of Compliance. EASA: **(EU) 2018/1974** + **ED Decision 2019/005/R** (RMT.0581) — advanced UPRT on aeroplane, −1g to 2.5/3g, FCL.745.A, FCL.915(e).

**Manual flying.** SAFO 13002 (4 Jan 2013), SAFO 17007 (4 May 2017), **AC 120-123 Flightpath Management** (21 Nov 2022) — the last explicitly proscribing blanket rules like "shall never manually fly at night," and treating manual flight as cognitive + psychomotor + communication skill. All advisory; the operational and economic pressures run the other way.

**CBTA generalises.** PANS-TRG is structured by personnel group: Part II flight crew (MPL, EBT, UPRT), cabin crew and dispatchers [Reserved], **Part III maintenance**, **Part IV ATM** (controllers, ATSEP) — with Doc 10056, Doc 10098 and Doc 10002 as supporting manuals. Amendment 7 (2020) formalised expansion to all Annex 1 licensing and Annex 6 operator training.

**The precondition.** Every number above exists because reporting was made safe. FOQA (AC 120-82) with de-identification, gatekeeper protocol, 49 U.S.C. §40123 / 14 CFR Part 193 / §13.401; ASAP (AC 120-66C) with the MOU, tripartite ERC, the "Big Five" exclusions, and "no action against an employee who submits a report that is accepted"; **ICAO Annex 19 2nd ed. (2016, applicable 7 Nov 2019)** §§5.1.2/5.1.3 and Appendix 3's prohibition on disciplinary/civil/criminal use with three narrow exceptions; **Regulation (EU) 376/2014** Art 2(12) just culture, Art 15 confidentiality, Art 16 non-prejudice plus the obligation on every organisation to "adopt internal rules describing how 'just culture' principles are guaranteed." Remove any of these and the data thins, at which point "evidence-based" reverts to expert opinion with a database attached.

**The live frontier.** IATA has flagged that CBTA creates a new class of individual performance data that Annex 13/19 protections do not cleanly cover, and has asked the ICAO Personnel Training and Licensing Panel to propose new Annex 1 and Annex 6 standards. **Any organisation importing competency-based assessment inherits that problem on day one.**

## Gaps and unverified items

**Corrections to the brief's assumptions**

- The **IATA Data Report is August 2014, 1st Edition — not 2013.**
- The 2020 competency change is **not a split** of Application of Procedures. One addition plus two renamings/expansions. OB numbering (0.1–0.7) confirms.
- **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/1387 is NOT the UPRT instrument** — it concerns landing performance and runway surface conditions.

**The most important gap is substantive, not bibliographic.** No independent, peer-reviewed evaluation demonstrating that EBT produces better safety outcomes than the Operator Proficiency Check it replaces. This should be stated in the report rather than smoothed over — it is the sharpest available illustration of the distinction between "evidence-derived curriculum" and "evidence of training effectiveness."
