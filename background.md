Organisations can prove people showed up. They struggle to prove people can do
the work. Closing that gap is the problem I keep returning to — and most of what
I have built, in one form or another, has been a way of closing it that holds up
under scrutiny.

I work as a competence-system architect rather than a technical assessor. I
design the assessment, calibration and verification layer; domain experts judge
the domain. The recurring move is to make judgement *defensible*: a common
standard external assessors calibrate to, assessment embedded inside accredited
work rather than bolted alongside it, and clear governance for who holds the
organisation side and who holds the candidate side.

Since 2022 I have worked independently from Stavanger, Norway. I have designed
senior leadership development for Ingka / IKEA across several leadership summits
(2024–2025), built a workforce capability framework from zero for Mori in Japan
(2025), and authored *Learning Value Architecture* (E-C-P-O) — a method for
connecting what an organisation says it develops to what it can evidence.

Before that I was a founding executive, one of three, of a new interdisciplinary
college at Zayed University in Abu Dhabi (2021–2022), where I designed *Partner
Challenge*: a first-year system in which external companies and faculty assess
students against live problems, with each partner's time capped so participation
stayed sustainable. Four years on, the university reported to the UN Principles
for Responsible Management Education that it had run 2,500+ students, 200+
partner organisations and 250+ external mentors, and had become a mandatory part
of the degree.

Earlier, as Regional Director for Europe at Minerva Project (Berlin, 2015–2019),
I opened its first European campus and authored an experiential lesson-plan
format later adopted across six further campuses — teams that did not report to
me, in cities I never worked in.

I hold a BA from the European College of Liberal Arts, Berlin (now Bard College
Berlin), and work in English, German and Romanian.
